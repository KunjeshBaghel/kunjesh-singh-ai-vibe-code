## DECISION: **NO TRADE** — stand down, preserve capital

**Five-view classification:** **Slightly Bearish → confirmed bearish break** (intraday).
**Volatility state (`strategy_ref_book.md` §8.5.1):** **CHEAP** (IVP < 30) with positive but thin VRP.
**Regime grid (§8.5.2) cell — Slightly/Strongly Bearish × CHEAP:** *"Small bear call spread only"* / *"Stand down. Cheap IV + bearish = the buyer's regime, not yours."*

### Why not just take a smaller position?

Because the arithmetic does not reach the objective at this risk profile. To net **₹6,000 (1% of ₹6L)** on the best available 1-DTE structure:

```
Best structure available:  NIFTY 18-Aug 24,400/24,500 Bear Call Spread
  SELL 24,400 CE @ 21.20 (bid)     BUY 24,500 CE @ 9.25 (ask)
  Net credit ≈ 11.75 pts  →  ₹763.75 per lot
  Max loss = (100 − 11.75) × 65    →  ₹5,736 per lot

To net ₹6,000 after charges you need ≈ 10–12 lots held near expiry
  →  MAX LOSS = 10 × ₹5,736  =  ₹57,360  =  9.6% of ₹6L on ONE trade
  →  Risk : reward ≈ 7.7 : 1

At a moderate-to-low 2% per-trade cap (₹12,000) the position is 2 lots:
  →  realistic net capture ≈ ₹700 – ₹1,200  =  0.12% – 0.20%
```

**There is not enough premium in the market today to pay 1% at a moderate-low risk budget.** Forcing the 1% requires ~10% single-trade risk. That is the trade that ends accounts, and it is exactly what the user's own brief says not to do.

### The specific thing that would hurt us

A short-vol position here is short the same gamma the professional desks spent Friday **buying**. Retail is long futures (+6,296) and short 1.22L puts — a crowded long that gets force-liquidated on a breakdown. With FII selling five sessions running and the support shelf being unwound live, the fat tail is a **downside acceleration with a VIX pop from an 11.3 floor**. Short premium at the 2nd IV percentile is the wrong side of that.
