## ★ The single most important lesson today

**A rising VIX does NOT invalidate a trend-aligned one-sided credit spread.**

I attached an abort condition — *"exit if VIX re-crosses 11.17"* — to a bear call spread. That rule
belongs to **neutral** structures (straddles, iron flies, condors) where rising vol hurts both wings.
On a **bear call spread with price falling away from the short strike, delta gain dominates vega
loss.** Obeying that abort would have exited at 11:23 for ~zero and forfeited the entire move.

**Corollary — a refinement to §8.12.6 earned today:**

> The compression-squeeze "trade nothing" veto applies to **NEUTRAL** premium selling, which loses
> whichever way the range breaks. A **ONE-SIDED** credit spread on the side the market is already
> leaning is the opposite trade: **the break is what pays it.**

Today the compression broke *downward* — the direction the bear call spread wanted. The veto was
right about the expansion and wrong about the harm, because the structure was on the correct side.

## Process

- **Test the structure the regime grid names, before testing any other.** The grid said
  *Slightly Bearish × CHEAP → "small bear call spread only."* At 10:30 I tested the §8.6.10 **iron
  fly** instead, it failed 2 of 4 filters, and I declared no-trade for the whole day. The spread —
  the grid's actual answer — was never priced until 11:00. A fly and a bear call spread are not
  interchangeable: the fly needs a pin, the spread only needs price not to rally 0.32%.

- **"Short vega is bad when VIX rises" is too coarse.** Rising VIX hurt the fly on both wings. On a
  one-sided bear call spread with price falling, delta gain dominates vega loss. Ask *which side the
  delta sits on* before letting a VIX print veto a structure.

- **§8.13 at 1-of-3 markers is "halve", not "stop".** Half size is what made 1% unreachable — the
  trade stayed permissible, the target did not. That is the correct trade-off, and it is worth
  stating explicitly so the size rule doesn't get quietly renegotiated to reach a headline number.

- **Write the abort conditions into the plan before entry.** Today's plan said *abort if VIX
  re-crosses 11.17*. It fired at 11:23. Pre-committed exits work; exits improvised at 11:23 do not.

## Analytical errors I made today (12:40 review)

- **Never state a position's P&L from the underlying — price both legs.** At 11:59 I said the
  declined spread "would be showing a profit" because SENSEX had fallen. Priced properly it was
  **−₹138/lot**: spot had bounced back above the entry and vega had risen. A bear call spread can be
  red on a red day.

- **Don't escalate the narrative ahead of the evidence.** At 11:59 I called it "compression beginning
  to resolve." It wasn't. Both indices probed within 18–50 pts of their range floors and **bounced**;
  today's 0.67% range is at the *upper end* of the recent 0.32–0.91% band, not outside it. The true
  state was *range grinding at its lower edge with vol bid* — low-edge, not high-danger.
  **A right answer reached for an overstated reason does not generalise to the next session.**

- **Corollary worth keeping:** the case for standing down was strong enough on its own terms
  (whipsaw −111 then +133 pts in 40 min on 0-DTE short gamma). It did not need dramatising.
