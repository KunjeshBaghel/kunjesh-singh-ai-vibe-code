## 11:20 — The ceiling calculation: what would 1% actually cost?

This is the number that settles the session.

```
NIFTY iron fly, straddle decays 6 pts by 2:30 (conservative):
   ₹234/lot  →  33 lots needed for ₹6,000 net
   max loss at 33 lots        = ₹1,48,005  =  24.7% of capital
   loss at the 1.5× stop      = ₹1,40,498  =  23.4% of capital     (cap: 1.0%)

NIFTY iron fly, straddle decays 10 pts (generous Friday markdown):
   ₹390/lot  →  20 lots needed for ₹6,000 net
   max loss at 20 lots        =   ₹89,700  =  14.9% of capital
   loss at the 1.5× stop      =   ₹85,150  =  14.2% of capital     (cap: 1.0%)
```

**Reaching 1% intraday today requires risking 14–23% of capital.** That is 14× to 23× the
per-trade cap, on a moderate-to-low risk profile.

**Stated structurally, so it generalises:** with a combined-premium stop at 2× credit, loss-at-stop
equals the credit. The ₹6,000 per-trade cap therefore caps *total credit* at about ₹6,000 —
so netting ₹6,000 would require capturing **more than 100% of the credit in one afternoon.**
100% of the credit only arrives at expiry, and expiry is two sessions away.

> **1% intraday and a 1% risk cap are mathematically incompatible for a credit structure.**
> A credit seller's intraday reward:risk is roughly 1:6 to 1:10. Getting 1:1 in a session needs
> either expiry-day pinning (no 0-DTE exists today) or a directional move, and buying direction
> into HV10 = 6.5% with a 0.34% day range is the wrong side of the trade.
