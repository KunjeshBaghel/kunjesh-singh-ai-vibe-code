## ★ The single most important lesson today

**The per-trade risk cap caps your maximum profit, not just your maximum loss — and the stop
multiple alone decides whether the day's target is arithmetically possible.**

```text
Combined-premium stop at k × credit  →  loss at stop = (k − 1) × credit
Risk cap R therefore bounds credit   →  MAX CREDIT = R / (k − 1)
Setting target T = R (1% target, 1% cap):

        REQUIRED CAPTURE OF THE CREDIT  =  (k − 1) × 100%

   k = 1.3 (intraday straddle)   →  30%   reachable
   k = 1.5 (0-DTE fly)           →  50%   reachable on expiry day
   k = 2.0 (weekly / monthly)    → 100%   ⛔ only exists at settlement
```

> **At k = 2.0 the 1% intraday target is not hard — it is impossible.** No structure, no strike, no
> index fixes it. I spent two hours pricing four candidates to rediscover a fact available from one
> division at 9:15.

**Written up as [§8.11.6 — the feasibility gate](../../../kb/kb1/strategy_ref_book.md#8116-the-feasibility-gate--can-todays-target-be-reached-at-all-added-28-aug-2026).**
Run it **before** chain analysis, not after.

### The uncomfortable corollary

A 1%-of-capital intraday target and a 1%-of-capital per-trade cap are compatible **on expiry day and
almost nowhere else** — and expiry day is exactly when short gamma is least manageable by hand.
**This tension is structural. It does not get resolved by raising size.**

This retro-explains the whole month: 24-Aug (1 DTE, gamma), 27-Aug (0-DTE fly failed filters, the
5-DTE alternative paid 0.23%), 28-Aug (2 DTE minimum, 0.04–0.13%). **Three no-trades, one cause.**

## Process

- **Fetch the expiry list first — it is the highest-information call of the session.** Today's entire
  verdict followed from *"nearest expiry is 2 trading sessions away, and BANKNIFTY has no weekly at
  all."* That was knowable at 9:15; I established it at 11:00. **Sessions to expiry, not calendar
  days** — 4 calendar days was 2 sessions.

- **When the target is unreachable, invert the calculation and quote the capital at risk.** Do not
  stop at "it doesn't reach 1%" — that invites negotiation on size. Say: *"it reaches 1% at 20–33
  lots, which is 14.9–24.7% of capital against a 1.0% cap."* **A ratio ends the conversation; an
  adjective does not.**

- **Measure decay, don't assume it.** I pulled Kite minute-bars on the 24,200 straddle rather than
  quoting a theta number: 194.10 → 190.20 over two hours. **3.9 points**, and that already included
  a vega tailwind (VIX 11.07 → 10.80). Any theoretical theta estimate would have been optimistic.

- **Separate "too dangerous" from "too small" in the write-up.** Both end in no-trade, but they
  generalise to opposite next actions: *too dangerous* → wait for the regime to change;
  **too small → wait for the calendar to change.** Conflating them would have made this session look
  like a third risk-off day and buried the real (and fixable) cause.

- **§8.12.6a held for the second time.** The grid named an iron fly; compression vetoed it *as a
  neutral structure*; the one-sided bull put spread stayed permitted and was priced properly rather
  than dismissed. The 27-Aug amendment did its job — the failure of a fly no longer kills the day.

## What the scoring actually proved

- **The iron fly's failure mode was delta, not theta.** The 24,250 straddle went 185.70 → **199.85**
  — it *expanded* — because spot drifted 54 pts away from the short strike. I modelled decay with
  care and outsourced direction to "the stop." **Direction is what decided it.**
  → When modelled edge (~₹150) is smaller than ordinary intraday drift in the same structure (~₹400),
  the trade isn't merely *too small* — **it is indistinguishable from noise.** Stronger and more
  useful than the framing I used at 11:25.

- **A 100-pt-OTM put at 2 DTE is a gamma instrument, not a theta instrument.** The 24,000 PE went
  **23.90 → 36.40 (+52%)** on a 54-point move. My "+₹263 to +₹823" assumed decay with spot roughly
  still, and never contemplated this.

- **§8.11.6 is validated by the day's best available outcome.** The only structure that worked —
  a 24250/24450 bear call spread — pays **+₹1,210 net at the 1 lot the cap permits = 0.20%**, almost
  exactly §8.11.5's ₹1,274 expectancy. **The ceiling really was ~0.2%.** No permitted structure was
  ever reaching ₹6,000, and the live tape confirms the gate's arithmetic rather than merely its mood.

- **That winner was not forecastable at 11:15** — price +63 over PDC, put writing 2× call writing,
  max pain overhead. §8.12.6a says lean *with* the market, and the market leaned mildly up.
  **The method picked the right structure for the read; the read was wrong.** Honest scoring means
  saying that, not retro-fitting a bear thesis I did not hold.

- **BANKNIFTY turned negative ~90 minutes before NIFTY followed** (−62 at 11:27 → −166 at 13:09,
  while NIFTY was still green). I called it "rotation, not direction." Worth watching as a
  higher-beta lead tell — **one observation, not yet a rule.** Log it again before trusting it.

### The finding that actually matters: the stop was inside the noise

The bull put spread printed **−9.60 at 11:45 — 95% of its stop distance, 30 minutes after entry** —
then finished **+1.25**. A near-stop-out that ends flat: full drawdown, a coin flip on being stopped
at the low, no payoff.

```
credit 10.10 · k=2.0  →  stop distance 10.10 pts  ( = 0.04% of spot )
24000 PE's own 30-min range that morning  ≈  ±12 pts
ratio 0.84×   →  the stop was narrower than one candle
```

**→ New §8.11.7.** This is a *third* stand-down reason, distinct from "too dangerous" and "too small":
**too thin to survive noise.** Far-OTM verticals at low IV are where it hides, because distance from
the money and distance to the stop move in **opposite** directions — the strike being far away is
exactly why the credit is thin.
