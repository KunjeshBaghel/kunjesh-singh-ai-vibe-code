### ★ THE LESSON — §10b is a symmetric test, and the structure is only asymmetric if the DIRECTION is right

*(Rewritten 03-Sep 16:03 on review. The first draft of this section is superseded — it argued for
relaxing §10b and priced only the winning side of its own argument. What it got right is kept; what
it got wrong is marked.)*

- ✅ **Correct, and it stands: §10b measures `|range|`. It is direction-agnostic.** That is exactly
  right for someone short both wings — a straddle, strangle or condor. The permitted structure here
  is a **one-sided vertical**, where travel away from the short strike is not risk, it is the payoff.
- ✅ **Correct: the measurement window was never the defect.** The 13:56 "opening-range contamination"
  hypothesis stays withdrawn — 09:15→11:05 gave 1.71, the full session gave 1.72.
- ⛔ **WRONG — "a symmetric vol test vetoes every high-RV day."** §10b is a **ratio**, not an absolute
  vol test. A high-RV day whose IV is higher still **passes** it. That day has a name in this book:
  §8.12.7, the VIX Spike Fade, *"the single best-paid seller setup."* §10b does not veto the best-paid
  days — it vetoes days where the tape is delivering more than the chain is charging for. Different
  claim, and much weaker.
- ⛔ **WRONG, and this is the dangerous one — the asymmetry is conditional on the direction call, and
  today the book did not have one.** A vertical is asymmetric **only if it is on the right side.** The
  five-view came out **SIDEWAYS**. This same file, four sections up, prices the Bull Put that cleared
  c/W, the credit floor and stop reachability **more easily than any Bear Call** and would have run to
  **−₹10,199, the full structural cap.** Same day. Same 1.7× realised. Opposite sign.

> **High RV × an unknown direction is symmetric risk in practice, whatever the payoff diagram says.**
> The first draft argued from the winning counterfactual and left the losing one — already written,
> already on the page — out of the argument. **That is n=1 twice: once in the sample, once in the
> selection.**

**What survives as the real, testable claim** — narrower than the first draft, and the only version
worth spending a backtest on:

> `UNPAID` may be too blunt **when the direction is independently confirmed**, and only then. Not
> "for one-sided verticals" — a one-sided vertical pointed the wrong way is what −₹10,199 looks like.

### On "why is only a one-sided vertical permitted?" — reviewed 03-Sep, the lock is upheld

The question came up while reviewing this lesson. Recording the answer here because the first draft
above **reasoned from the lock as though it were a claim about markets**, and it is not one.

- **Every locked structure fails an operator-side constraint, not a market test:** manual 4-fill
  execution in a phone app with two hand-placed stops · the ₹10,500 structural cap at **one lot**
  (`TC §3`) · no Greeks feed, where `c/W ≈ Δ` is the only substitute and it exists **only for a two-leg
  vertical** (`TC §14`) · intraday-only (SI-7) killing calendars · and the permanent ban on any
  structure that increases short exposure while losing.
- **The day's data cannot upgrade execution capacity at 10:45 AM.** A structure menu is a *capability*
  question, reviewed on a schedule against a sample. The day's data picks **from** the menu; it never
  expands it. Let the tape open the menu and the tape will find a reason every session — 24-Jul
  (condor → iron fly, −₹1,965) and 01-Sep (8 lots, −₹15,564) are what that looks like from the inside.
- **It is already conditional, not doctrine.** `TC §11` unlocks the 4-leg family at 30 clean verticals.
  Current qualification: **one recorded stop reached, zero honoured.**

**Two defects found in how the lock is WRITTEN** — both real, both for the user to rule on, neither
touched today:

1. **Jade Lizard and BWB are banned for a property of one parameterisation.** `§8.6` locks them under
   *"exceeds the ₹10,500 structural cap at ONE lot"* — but that is the worked example's width, not the
   structure. Both can be built narrower. They are also **3-leg**, so `TC §11`'s 4-leg unlock row does
   not cover them: **they are the only locked family in the book with no key at all.** A lock with no
   key is a ban wearing a probation's clothes.
2. **`TC §5` states the permitted list as "That is the entire list" with no rationale.** Written that
   way it reads as a market claim, which is precisely how the first draft of the lesson above came to
   be written. **Proposed wording: state the constraint that produced each lock, so the next reader
   cannot mistake a capacity limit for an edge.** No number changes.
