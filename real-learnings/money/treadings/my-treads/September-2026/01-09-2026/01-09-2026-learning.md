## L1 — Pro desk short calls = CEILING, not just a range signal

**What happened:** Pro desk held −1,09,003 net short calls on 31-Aug (T-1). We read this as "range signal" and applied §8.5.4 PE-first → Bull Put Spread. The correct read was: Pro has defined a HARD CEILING at 24,100–24,200. On a day with a ceiling, the market either stays below the ceiling OR drifts lower — both scenarios favour a Bear Call Spread AT the ceiling, not a Bull Put Spread near the floor.

**Rule:** If Pro desk has >50,000 net short calls at a specific strike, that strike is the ceiling for the session. Default structure = Bear Call Spread at or just above that strike. Do NOT sell puts expecting the market to hover near the ceiling — when the ceiling holds, the market gravitates downward.

**Why this matters:** The Bear Call Spread 24,100/24,300 was actually recommended at 10:29 and was correct. The switch to Bull Put Spread (triggered by expert feedback + §8.5.4) was the structural error.

## L2 — Pre-set triggers must be broker alerts, not mental notes

**What happened:** At 13:05 the monitoring report set a clear exit trigger: "if 24,000 PE crosses ₹11.00 → exit immediately." At 13:11, the user SOLD a 9th lot at ₹11.45 (adding to position) instead of exiting. At 13:12 Claude called exit (PE ₹12.05). Position was not closed until 14:51 @ ₹35.90.

**Rule:** Every time a trigger price is set in a monitoring report, immediately set a price alert in Kotak/Kite app before doing anything else. Do not rely on watching manually. A trigger not set in the broker app does not exist.

## L3 — Never add to a short option position on expiry day after noon

**What happened:** At 13:11 (PE already at ₹11.45, past the ₹11.00 trigger), the user added a 9th lot short. This increased loss exposure by ₹1,430 and delayed exit psychology ("maybe it will recover now that I added").

**Rule:** On expiry day after 12:00 PM, the only two choices for a losing short option are EXIT or HOLD. Adding is never an option. Adding to a losing short on expiry day = averaging into a position with exponential gamma risk.

## L4 — OI wall drop >20% in one monitoring window = immediate exit

**What happened:**
- 13:05: 24,000 PE OI = 57.76M (building — seemed safe)
- 13:55: 24,000 PE OI = 32.80M (−43% in 50 min — wall collapsed)

The collapse from 57M to 33M meant institutional put writers had fully covered. Once the wall was gone, NIFTY had nothing anchoring it at 24,000. The move from 24,080 → 23,990 → deeper was the predictable consequence.

**Rule:** At every monitoring check, compare current OI to the previous check (not to prior day). If the short-strike PE wall drops >20% in one window → exit immediately regardless of other signals. The floor is gone.

## L5 — Dhan MCP OAuth expires mid-session — use REST API as default for chains

**What happened:** Dhan MCP returned Unauthorized repeatedly after initial auth, even after multiple successful OAuth completions. Three re-login cycles cost 15+ minutes of trading time. REST API with `DHAN_ACCESS_TOKEN` worked reliably throughout the session.

**Rule:** For all option chain data fetches during a live session, use `curl` + `DHAN_ACCESS_TOKEN` (REST API) directly. Do NOT depend on Dhan MCP OAuth. The MCP login can be attempted once at session start; if it fails, switch to REST immediately and don't retry.

## L7 — §8.5.4 PE-first principle has a boundary condition

§8.5.4 says "default to PE selling (puts are expensive due to skew) — override to CE selling only for explicitly bearish views with call OI wall visible."

**Boundary:** When the Pro desk has a massive net short call position AND the session is expiry day AND NIFTY is sitting just below the call OI wall, the correct read is that the call wall is the dominant force, not the put skew. In this case, §8.5.4 should be overridden in favour of selling CE at the wall even with a neutral/sideways view.

**Refined rule:** If Pro desk net short calls > 80,000 AND a visible call OI wall exists at the same level → use Bear Call Spread regardless of §8.5.4, because the ceiling is the primary market structure for the session.
