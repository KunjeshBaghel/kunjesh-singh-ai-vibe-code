The stock market is a public marketplace where people buy and sell small shares of ownership in publicly listed companies. In India, it functions like a giant digital market where businesses raise capital to expand, and everyday individuals buy ownership stakes to build long-term wealth.

**How It Works (The *Sabzi Mandi* Analogy)**

Think of the stock market like a local vegetable market, but instead of trading produce, you trade tiny pieces of businesses like Tata, Reliance, or Infosys.

* **Stock / Share:** A digital token showing you own a tiny fraction of a company. If the company makes higher profits and expands, the value of your share increases.
* **Supply & Demand:** Prices fluctuate constantly based on sentiment. If a company announces strong profits, more people want to buy, pushing the stock price up. If news is bad, owners sell, pushing the price down.

**Key Players in the Indian Market**

| Entity | What It Is | Everyday Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Stock Exchanges** | Digital trading platforms | **NSE** (National Stock Exchange) and **BSE** (Bombay Stock Exchange) are the marketplaces where buyers and sellers meet. |
| **Market Indices** | Performance barometers | **Nifty 50** (top 50 companies on NSE) and **Sensex** (top 30 on BSE) show whether the overall Indian economy is trending up or down. |
| **SEBI** | The market referee | The Securities and Exchange Board of India enforces rules to prevent fraud and protect retail investors. |
| **Stockbrokers** | Authorized intermediaries | Platforms like Zerodha, Groww, or Angel One that allow you to execute trades on the exchanges. |

**How You Make Money**

* **Capital Gains:** You buy a stock at ₹100. As the business grows, the price reaches ₹160. Selling it yields a ₹60 profit per share.
* **Dividends:** Profits shared directly with shareholders as cash payouts deposited into your bank account.
* **Risk:** If a business fails or market conditions worsen, share prices fall, causing your investment to lose value.

**Getting Started in India**

To invest in India, you need three linked accounts: a **Bank Account** (to transfer funds), a **Trading Account** (to place orders), and a **Demat Account** (an electronic locker to store your shares). Beginners often start with low-cost Nifty 50 index funds to invest across India's top 50 companies at once rather than picking individual stocks.
